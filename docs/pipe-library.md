# Pipe catalogues

The pipe panel offers four catalogues besides a pipe of given diameter: carbon steel, stainless steel, HDPE and ductile iron. A catalogue gives the inner diameter of a pipe from its nominal size and series, and a new material sets the roughness of the pipe. The tables are `src/core/data/steel-pipe.js`, `stainless-pipe.js`, `hdpe-pipe.js` and `ductile-iron-pipe.js`, and each registers its catalogue in `src/core/data/pipe-library.js` (`PumpIt.data.registerPipes`, `pipeDims`, `fitMaterial`...; the contract of a catalogue is at the top of that file), so a new material is one more file (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, *A new pipe catalogue*). The designations shown in the interface come from `src/ui/common/pipe-labels.js`; the checks are the "Pipe catalogues" sections of `tests/run-tests.js` (tables and changes of material) and `tests/check-i18n.js` (texts).

| Ductile iron, cement mortar lining | ABNT NBR 7675, ISO 2531 | DN 80 to 1200 | K7, K9 | 0.1 mm |

## Decisions

**P-01 Sources.** The dimensions come from the standards above. They are sold documents, so every table was checked against public reproductions of them: table 4.B.1 of the Hydraulic Institute (HI Data Tool, ASME B36.19M-2018) for stainless steel, row by row; for HDPE, the ISO 4437 table of SDR 17 and SDR 11 published by Engineering ToolBox, the SINAPI items of NBR 15561 pipes (PE 100 SDR 11) and the datasheets of PE 100 pipe makers (44 published walls, all equal to the table); for ductile iron, the datasheets of a Brazilian maker that cite NBR 7675:2022 (outside diameters, K9 walls, lining) and the tables of IS 8329, derived from ISO 2531:1998 (outside diameters, the minimum walls of K7 and K9). The roughness comes from Moody (1944), White (*Fluid Mechanics*, table 6.1), the Hydraulic Institute (*Engineering Data Book*, table 3.A.1 of the HI Data Tool) and the lining data of Saint-Gobain PAM. No file, database or screen of any hydraulic program was used.

**P-02 Stainless steel.** ASME B36.19M-2018 has the outside diameters of ASME B36.10M and its own schedules: 5S from NPS 1/2, and only 5S and 10S at NPS 22 and 30. The bore is the outside diameter less two nominal walls, as for carbon steel.

**P-03 HDPE.** The nominal size is the outside diameter in millimetres (20 to 1600 mm, the sizes of NBR 15561 and ISO 4427-2), and the series is the SDR. The walls are the minimum walls of the universal table of ISO 4065: e = d/SDR with the exact S of the R10 series (SDR = 2S + 1; S 5 is 10<sup>0.7</sup> = 5.0119), rounded to 0.01 mm and then up to 0.1 mm, with 2.0 mm at least and 3.0 mm for 32 mm SDR 11, as the PE pipe standards give. A series starts at the first outside diameter whose d/SDR reaches 1.8 mm (32 mm for SDR 17, 75 mm for SDR 41), which is where the published tables start; the thickest walls of the largest sizes follow the same rule and may not be made. The bore is the outside diameter less two minimum walls, as the tables of the standard give it. The wall tolerance is positive (up to about a tenth of the wall), so the bore of a real pipe is somewhat smaller, by up to about 1 %; the *diameter reduction* field can take it. The compound only sets the nominal pressure shown with the SDR (P-08).

**P-04 Ductile iron.** The DN range is that of NBR 7675 (80 to 1200; ISO 2531 also has DN 40 to 2600). The outside diameter depends only on the DN. The nominal iron wall of the thickness classes is e = K·(0.5 + 0.001·DN), rounded to 0.1 mm, with 5 mm at least for K7 and 6 mm for K9 (ISO 2531:1998, which NBR 7675 follows; the newer pressure classes C25 to C100 of ISO 2531:2009 are not in the catalogue). The nominal cement mortar lining of NBR 7675:2022 is 3 mm up to DN 300, 5 mm up to DN 600 and 6 mm up to DN 1200 (the mean minimums are 2.5, 4.5 and 5.5 mm). The bore is the outside diameter less two nominal walls and two nominal linings: DN 100 K9 gives 100.0 mm, DN 400 K9 402.8 mm.

**P-05 Carbon steel.** Unchanged: ASME B36.10M, NPS 1/2 to 24, Sch 40, 80, STD and XS, the default of a new pipe. Its bore now comes from the same rule as the other catalogues, and the inner diameter field takes it rounded to 0.01 mm from the exact value: 8" Sch 80 and XS give 193.68 mm (exactly 193.675 mm), where the former code gave 193.67 through the binary rounding of the product. No example uses that size, and a saved model keeps the diameter it holds until its size or series is chosen again.

**P-06 Roughness.** Choosing a material gives the pipe the absolute roughness of a new pipe of that material:

- Carbon steel 0.046 mm, new commercial steel (Moody).
- Stainless steel 0.015 mm, the value of the Hydraulic Institute, which also gives it for every schedule of ASME B36.19M pipe. Other tables give 0.0015 to 0.045 mm, depending on the finish. The Hydraulic Institute advises adding 15 % for commercial installations; this is not applied.
- HDPE 0.0015 mm, drawn tubing and plastics (Moody; the Hydraulic Institute lists plastic with drawn tubing).
- Ductile iron 0.1 mm, the value that the maker of the lined pipes advises for design: it covers the joints and the other singular losses of a line. A single new pipe measures about 0.03 mm, as do field calibrations of lined pipe (0.024 to 0.040 mm).

The list of typical values gains stainless steel and both values of lined ductile iron. "Drawn tubing" no longer names stainless steel, and "cast iron" is now called unlined, which is the cast iron of Moody's 0.26 mm.

**P-07 Changing the material, size or series.** A new material keeps the nominal size when both catalogues use the same sizes (carbon and stainless steel, by NPS). Otherwise it takes the default series (stainless 10S, HDPE SDR 11 PE 100, ductile iron K9) and the size whose bore is closest to the pipe's: from 4" Sch 40 (102.26 mm), HDPE OD 125 SDR 11 (102.2 mm) or ductile iron DN 100 (100.0 mm). It also resets the friction to its roughness, as carbon steel did. A size without the series in use takes the nearest series in the order of the catalogue, the heavier one on a tie. Every change of size or series sets the inner diameter again, rounded to 0.01 mm as before.

**P-08 Designations.** The results table shows the nominal size and the series of a catalogue pipe (4" and Sch. 40, OD 110 and SDR 11, DN 200 and K9; *DE* in Portuguese and Spanish), so its column is now called *Schedule / class*. The designations follow the kind of nominal size (`system`: inches, outside diameter or DN) and of series (`seriesKind`: schedule, SDR or class) of each catalogue, not the material, and the list of series shows the nominal pressure of each one in a catalogue with grades (SDR 11 · PN 16). The right-click card adds the material and the whole designation, with the compound and the nominal pressure of HDPE, as does the model data table. The nominal pressure is PN = 20·σ/(SDR − 1), with the design stress σ = MRS/1.25 of ISO 4427-1 (8.0 MPa for PE 100, 6.3 MPa for PE 80), in the PN series of the pipe standards (PE 100 SDR 26 is PN 6, PE 80 SDR 41 is PN 3.2). It is only a label: the calculation does not compare pressures with it. The results of the calculation no longer carry the NPS and schedule of carbon steel; the interface reads the material of the pipe.
